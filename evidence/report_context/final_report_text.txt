Abstract 
Agricultural forecasting requires consistent annual targets, 
chronological evaluation and transparent treatment of 
incomplete reporting. This study analyzes 246,091 crop -
production records covering 1997 –2015 and constructs 
annual production -to-area ratios after conservative quality 
checks. A matched 2005 –2014 panel contains 1,112 
district–crop histories for Rice, Maize, Wheat and 
Rapeseed &Mustard. Eight local baseline or classical 
estimators are compared with a globally tuned XGBoost 
model, LSTM, GRU and a  lightweight patch -based 
Transformer. Twelve XGBoost candidates use expanding 
validation targets in 2010 –2011; neural epoch selection 
uses an earlier 2009 stopping year. The validation -selected 
XGBoost achieves one -step test MAE 0.3369, RMSE 
0.5617 and MAPE 24.77% over 2012–2014. LSTM has the 
lowest retrospective test RMSE, 0.5457, while Naive has 
the lowest MAPE. Fixed -origin three -step results are 
reported separately. Prophet was implemented for longer 
crop-level aggregates but its native optimizer failed  locally, 
so no Prophet accuracy is asserted. Source units, district 
boundaries and cohort selection limit interpretation. This 
retrospective extension provides reproducible evidence for 
model comparison rather than guaranteed operational 
accuracy or an independent prospective validation. 
Keywords: agricultural forecasting, annual panel, XGBoost, 
LSTM, GRU, patch Transformer, chronological validation. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Agricultural planners need forecasts whose target definition 
and information set remain clear. A district -level yield 
forecast can support investigation and production planning, 
but reported accuracy is misleading when aggregation 
combines overlapping seasonal records or when future 
values enter model features. Short annual histories also 
make persistence a demanding benchmark for more 
complex methods. 
The objective is to compare classical and advanced 
methods on common targets while preserving an auditable 
progression from raw records to predictions. The 
experiment uses crop_production.csv, with seven fields and 
246,091 observations. It extends the existing ten -year study 
with a larger chronological XGBoost search and genuinely 
trained small neural models. The contribution is a 
reproducible application and evidence audit; no novel 
architecture or universal forecasting superiority is claimed. 
The extension keeps model selection separate from 
retrospective test ranking. Its test years were already 
examined in an earlier project version. Therefore, the 
present results are retrospective benchmark evidence, not 
an independently blinded or prospective holdout. The 
report states this limitation and does not substitute a test 
winner for the validation-selected method. 
II. LITERATURE REVIEW 
Statistical forecasting. Hyndman and Athanasopoulos [2] 
explain baseline methods and out -of-sample evaluation, 
while Box et al. [3] establish autoregressive modeling. 
Their relevance is the need to define a forecast origin and 
compare complex models against simple alternatives. This 
experiment includes persistence, historical mean, moving 
average, drift and an explicitly labeled OLS AR(1). 
Exponential smoothing. Hyndman et al. [4] provide a state -
space framework for smoothing. This study uses custom 
conditional least -squares SES, Holt and damped Holt 
recursions. They share the underlying level/trend ideas but 
are not claimed to reproduce full likelihood -based ETS 
estimation or a specific library implementation. 
Agricultural machine learning. Van Klompenburg et al. [5] 
review crop -yield prediction and frequently used 
environmental inputs. Temperature, rainfall, soil and 
management variables are absent here. The present 
comparison concerns historical predictability; its observed 
correlations and yield -ratio declines cannot establish 
agricultural causes. 
Boosting. Chen and Guestrin [6] describe regularized tree 
boosting in XGBoost. A global regressor shares lag –target 
relationships across districts and crops. Chronological 
candidate evaluation is required because nonlinear capacity 
does not guarantee better generalization on annual histories. 
Sequence models. LSTM [7] and the recurrent encoder –
decoder of Cho et al. [8] motivate gated recurrent models. 
Nie et al. [10] motivate patching and channel -independent 
temporal attention. The current patch -based model is a 
small inspired implementation, not an exact PatchTST 
reproduction. Prophet [9] motivates a separate trend model 
with unjustified seasonal components disabled; its local 
optimizer failure is an execution finding, not evidence that 
the method is intrinsically unsuitable.

B. Conservative annual aggregation 
Categorical whitespace is standardized. Missing production 
is not imputed. Nonfinite measures, nonpositive area and 
negative production invalidate a component. An annual 
state–district–crop group is excluded when any selected 
component is invalid, a seasonal business key is duplicated, 
or Whole Year overlaps another season. The original input 
has no Total category. No outliers are automatically 
removed. 
The accepted annual target is: 
Y(G) = Σ(r∈G) Production(r) / Σ(r∈G) Area(r).       (1) 
Equation (1) is an area -weighted aggregation, not an 
average of individual ratios. Accepted annual groups 
account for 241,302 raw rows; invalid or missing -
component groups account for 3,997 rows; overlapping 
Whole Year groups account for 792 rows. These decisions 
sum to the raw 246,091 records. Nonpositive -area and 
negative-production counts are both zero. Recorded -versus-
calculated yield discrepancy testing is inapplicable because 
the file has no recorded yield column.

B. Baselines and classical estimators 
Naive repeats the latest observation; Mean averages the 
history; MA3 averages its latest three values; Drift 
extrapolates the average first -to-last change. AR1_OLS 
estimates an intercept and one lag by unconstrained least 
squares. Its multi-step forecast recursively applies the fitted 
coefficients, which can be unstable on short or nearly 
constant histories. 
SES_LS updates level as ℓ(t)=αy(t)+(1−α)ℓ(t−1). Holt_LS 
adds trend; DampedHolt_LS damps it by φ. Conditional 
one-step SSE is minimized with L -BFGS-B. Initial level is 
the first observation; initial trend is the first difference, 
except SES uses zero. α and β lie in [0.01,0.99] and 
damping in [0.8,0.98]. Fixed -origin forecasts use one fitted 
state, without refitting to synthetic predictions. The LS 
labels distinguish these models from full ETS. Statsmodels 
ARIMA imports remain blocked by local application -
control policy; no ARIMA accuracy is reported. 
C. Expanded XGBoost tuning 
A global XGBoost regressor uses yield lags 1 –3, their 
trailing mean, their population standard deviation and year 
minus 2005. Current production, current area and future 
targets are excluded. No geographic or crop encoding is 
added, preserving the existing feature definition. Twelve 
candidates comprise the two earlier settings and ten seeded 
combinations of estimators, depth, learning rate, 
row/column sampling and regularization. Selection uses 
pooled validation RMSE only. 
 
Fig. 4. Actual expanding -validation RMSE across twelve XGBoost 
candidates. Expanding the search did not produce a better validation 
setting than the earlier selected candidate. 
The winning candidate remains trial 1: 250 estimators, 
depth 3, learning rate 0.03, subsample 0.8, column 
sampling 0.9 and L2 penalty 10. The additional candidates 
vary estimator count among 100/200/350, depth 2/3/4, 
learning rate 0.03/0.05/0.08, row and column sampling, L1 
penalty and minimum child weight. Every candidate 
configuration and score is exported. No extra improvement 
is fabricated merely because the search budget increased. 
D. Small recurrent and patch-based models 
The global LSTM and GRU use one recurrent layer with 16 
hidden units, a final -state linear head and dropout 0.1. 
PatchTransformerTiny uses a three -year input, two 
overlapping length-two patches, an eight-dimensional patch 
embedding, learned positional information, two attention 
heads, one pre -normalized encoder layer, a 16 -unit feed -
forward block, dropout 0.1 and a flattened linear 
forecasting head. It is a lightweight patch -based model 
inspired by [10], not an exact PatchTST reproduction.

E. Chronological evaluation and metrics 
The common modeled window is 2005 –2014. Outer 
validation targets are 2010 and 2011 with origins 2009 and 
2010. Selection freezes on validation RMSE. Final pre -test 
fitting uses 2005–2011. An expanding one-step test refits at 
origins 2011, 2012 and 2013; a separate fixed -origin test 
uses only information through 2011 for all three 2012–2014 
horizons. Each model has 2,224 validation targets and 
3,336 targets per test protocol. Supervised global training 
starts in 2008 because three historical values are required. 
For N aligned targets, e(i)=y(i)−ŷ(i): 
MAE = N⁻¹ Σ |e(i)|.                                               (2) 
RMSE = sqrt[N⁻¹ Σ e(i)²].                                     (3) 
MAPE = 100 |S|⁻¹ Σ(i∈S) |e(i)/y(i)|.                   (4) 
S excludes |y|≤10⁻⁸; one of the 3,336 test targets is excluded 
from MAPE. MAE and RMSE retain all targets. Scores 
weight district/year observations equally. Additional 
exports include sMAPE, MASE and signed mean error. 
MASE uses training first differences from 2005 –2009 for 
validation and 2005–2011 for testing; undefined zero scales 
are omitted. Models enter the leaderboard only when all 
common targets have finite predictions. Seeds, versions, 
timings, fit statuses and checkpoints are recorded.

VI. LIMITATIONS AND CHALLENGES 
The individual histories are short and the validation/test 
periods contain few independent years. Shared shocks 
reduce temporal replication despite many districts. 
Complete-history selection excludes changing or 
incomplete regions and depends on test -era availability. 
The test years were examined previously, so the extension 
is retrospective and cannot support independent prospective 
generalization claims. 
Units, exact source lineage/license and stable district 
boundaries remain unresolved. Production missingness and 
seasonal overlap exclusions may be nonrandom. Aggregate 
ratios can change with area weights and reporting coverage. 
The study concerns four crops and cannot establish 
performance for all 124 crop labels. No causal agricultural 
explanation follows without environmental and 
management variables. 
The neural architectures are deliberately small and their 
three-year context limits long -range dependence learning. 
They are not extensively tuned. Early stopping uses a single 
inner year; expanded XGBoost tuning still has a bounded 
12-candidate budget. No significance test establishes that 
the small score gaps persist elsewhere. Prophet and 
ARIMA execution limitations are explicit, and probabilistic 
calibration remains future work. 
The final notebook executes in a real local Jupyter kernel. 
The Colab browser is signed out, so hosted execution 
cannot be certified. Dependency installation and upload 
adapter behavior are tested locally and the code uses CPU. 
Official faculty milestone PDFs were unavailable; exact 
institutional compliance and individual contribution 
evidence therefore remain unverified. 
VII. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 
The study completed raw -data auditing, annual 
aggregation, time series EDA, pre -test diagnostics, 12 -
candidate XGBoost tuning and genuine LSTM, GRU and 
patch-based Transformer training on a common 1,112 -
series cohort. Validation selects XGBoost_Tuned; its one -
step test MAE is 0.3369, RMSE 0.5617 and MAPE 
24.77%. LSTM has the lowest retrospective RMSE, while 
metric and horizon rankings differ. These distinctions are 
retained rather than selecting a winner from the test set. 
For farmers, insurers and planners, the outputs can serve as 
investigated baselines after domain validation, not 
guaranteed recommendations. Future work should verify 
provenance and units, obtain stable geographic crosswalks, 
add weather/soil/satellite variables, evaluate genuinely new 
years, and calibrate multi -horizon uncertainty. More 
observations and prospective evaluation would support 
stronger claims than increasing model complexity alone.
